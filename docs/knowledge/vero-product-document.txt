The Six Layer Scan
Layer 1 - Corporate Foundation Scans: Entity registration, director records, cap table documents, board resolutions, constitutional documents. Flags: Wrong entity type for fundraising stage, missing director filings, undocumented equity, expired board resolutions, missing foreign qualifications for entities operating cross-border.
Layer 2 - Intellectual Property and Technology Protection Scans: IP assignment documents, contractor agreements, software licence records, trademark registrations, open source usage declarations. Flags: Contractors who built core product without IP assignment, missing founder IP transfer, open source licences creating copyleft risk, unregistered trademarks in operating markets, AI model rights undocumented.
Layer 3 - Compliance and Regulatory Posture Scans: Regulatory registrations, privacy policies, data processing records, sector-specific licences, AI governance documentation. Flags: NDPR registration missing, GDPR privacy policy absent or outdated, no DPA template for vendors, CBN or FCA licence required but not held, EU AI Act obligations unaddressed, CCPA notice missing for California users.
Layer 4 - Commercial Infrastructure Scans: MSA template, SaaS terms, API agreements, DPAs, vendor contracts, employment agreements. Flags: No MSA in place, SaaS terms missing key liability caps, no DPA with cloud vendors, employment agreements without IP assignment, no API access agreement governing third party integrations.
Layer 5 - Fundraising Scans: Cap table documentation, SAFE and SAFT records, shareholder agreements, investor rights documentation, data room organisation. Flags: Cap table not reconciled, SAFE conversion mechanics inconsistent with shareholder agreement, missing drag-along provisions, undocumented advisor equity, no investor data room in place.
Layer 6 - AI and Data Governance Scans: AI model usage agreements, training data licences, AI governance policy, algorithmic accountability documentation, data processing agreements specific to AI. Flags: No AI model usage agreement, training data licence absent, no AI acceptable use policy, GDPR Article 22 automated decision-making obligations unaddressed, EU AI Act risk classification not completed.


What The Output Looks Like
The Legal Health Score - a number out of 100. Simple. Like a credit score. Updated every time they add a document or complete a scan.
The Six Layer Breakdown - red, amber, green per layer with a sub-score. A technology company can see immediately which layer is strongest and which needs urgent attention.
The Gap Report - every red and amber item described in plain language. What is missing, why it matters, what happens if it stays missing - expressed as a business consequence not a legal abstraction.
"You do not have an IP assignment agreement from your lead developer who built the core product between January 2023 and August 2024. This means your company may not legally own the technology it is selling. This will be flagged in your first investor due diligence and will need to be resolved before any round can close."
The Priority Action List - the top five things to fix ranked by severity and cost of delay. Each item has a direct action - Quick Order a document, book a consultation, complete a regulatory registration.
The Verification Badge - once a company reaches a score above a threshold and Vero has reviewed the key documents, they get a verified Legal Health badge. Like a security certificate for a website. They can display it to investors, enterprise clients and partners as proof their legal infrastructure has been professionally reviewed.


How The Scan Works
Step 1 - Company Connect The company logs in and connects their basic information. Company name, jurisdiction, stage, sector, operating markets. This takes two minutes and gives the platform enough to run the public data layer of the scan immediately.
Step 2 - Public Data Scan The platform automatically checks publicly available data. CAC registration status. Companies House filings. Delaware Secretary of State records. USPTO and UKIPO trademark filings. ICO registration. Published privacy policy on their website. This happens automatically without the company doing anything.
Step 3 - Document Upload The company uploads their key documents into a structured vault - organised by the six layers. Each slot tells them exactly what goes there and why. They do not need to know what documents they should have - the vault tells them. Empty slots are automatically flagged as gaps.
Step 4 - Product Questionnaire A short structured questionnaire about what their product does. Do you process personal data? Do you use AI in your product? Do you process payments? Do you operate in regulated sectors? Do you have users in the EU, UK, US or Nigeria? Each answer triggers specific legal obligation checks across the relevant jurisdictions.
Step 5 - Score Generation The platform generates the Legal Health Score and full six layer report immediately based on what it knows. The score updates in real time as documents are added and gaps are resolved.
Step 6 - Vero Verification For companies on a paid plan, a lawyer reviews the uploaded documents and verifies that what is there is actually sufficient - not just present. An AI tool cannot tell you if your SAFE agreement has an inconsistent conversion mechanic. A lawyer can. This is the accountability layer that makes Vero’'s product fundamentally different from any automated tool.

What Vero Scan Is And What It Is Not
What it is:
A legal credit score. An at-a-glance signal. The same way a financial credit score does not replace an audit but tells you instantly whether a company's financial behaviour is healthy, trustworthy and fundable - a Legal Health Score does the same thing for legal infrastructure.
A company either has IP assignment from their contractors or they do not. They either have NDPR registration or they do not. Their SAFE agreement either works with their cap table structure or it has a problem. Their privacy policy either covers their actual data flows or it does not.
These are not matters of legal opinion. They are matters of fact. The scan establishes the facts. The score reflects them. Every company now has a legal credit score - visible, trackable, improvable over time.
What it is not:
It is not legal advice. It is not a full legal review. It does not replace a lawyer reviewing transaction documents before a round closes or a lawyer doing full due diligence before an acquisition. That full review is still Code & Clause's core service - and critically, the Legal Health Score creates the demand for that full review by showing companies and investors exactly where the gaps are.
The score is the diagnostic. Code & Clause is the treatment.

The Jurisdiction Architecture
How it will work
When a company or investor starts a scan, they select:
Where the company is incorporated - single selection. This determines the corporate foundation layer. Different registry, different filing requirements, different corporate law.
Where the company operates and has users - multiple selection by country. This determines the compliance and regulatory layer. Each country selected adds its specific regulatory obligations to the scan.
Where their investors are based - multiple selection. This determines the fundraising readiness layer. A Nigerian company with US investors needs SAFE mechanics that work under both Nigerian company law and US securities regulation. A company with UK investors needs documents that satisfy English law requirements.
What sector they operate in - this determines which sector-specific regulatory frameworks apply within each selected country.




The Country Registry Layer
Important structural decision. For each country of incorporation, the platform connects to or references the relevant company registry to verify the company's existence, structure and filing status.
You do not need to build API connections to every registry on day one. For the pilot you reference them. For version two you connect to the ones that have accessible APIs. For version three you build or partner for the ones that require proprietary access.
The registry map by country - starting with your core markets:
Nigeria - Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC). Public registry. CAC search API exists. Company name, RC number, directors, share structure, filing status all verifiable.
United Kingdom - Companies House. Full public API. One of the best registry APIs in the world. Director history, filing history, PSC register, accounts, charges - all accessible.
United States - varies by state. Delaware Secretary of State for most startups. OpenCorporates aggregates US state registries. EDGAR for SEC-registered companies. USPTO for trademark and patent filings. PACER for federal court records.
Germany - Handelsregister. Public access. Bundesanzeiger for financial disclosures.
France - INPI and Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés. Public API access.
Netherlands - Kamer van Koophandel (KvK). API available.
Spain - Registro Mercantil. Your GPS Patron client was a Spanish company - you already know this registry. Futura and similar structures registered here.
Estonia - e-Business Register. One of the most advanced registry APIs in the world. Highly relevant for European tech startups using the e-Residency programme.
British Virgin Islands - BVI Financial Services Commission. Relevant for holding structures. Less public data but verifiable through registered agent requirements.
Cayman Islands - General Registry. Similar to BVI. Relevant for fund structures.
African countries - starting with the highest startup activity:
Nigeria - CAC as above.
Kenya - Business Registration Service. Public search available.
Ghana - Registrar General's Department. Accessible.
South Africa - Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Public search API.
Egypt - Commercial Register. Accessible with limitations.
Rwanda - Rwanda Development Board. Accessible.
Senegal - APIX. Accessible.
Ethiopia - Ethiopian Investment Commission. Limited digital access - manual verification for now.
Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia - all have registries with varying levels of digital accessibility.
Other startup-heavy markets:
India - Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA21). Excellent API.
Singapore - Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority (ACRA). Excellent API.
Canada - Corporations Canada. Accessible.
Australia - ASIC. Accessible.
Israel - Companies Registrar. Accessible.
UAE/Dubai - Department of Economic Development. Accessible with limitations.
Brazil - Junta Comercial varies by state. OpenCorporates covers partial access.


How The Country Selection Drives The Report
When a company selects their countries, the platform builds their specific legal obligation map automatically. Here is what that looks like in practice:
A Nigerian fintech company incorporated in Nigeria, operating in Nigeria and Kenya, with users in the UK and Germany, and investors based in the US and UK - their Legal Health Report covers:
Corporate Foundation: CAC registration and filing compliance.
IP Protection: Nigerian Copyright Act 2022, Patents and Designs Act, NOTAP registration for technology transfer.
Compliance: CBN licensing for payments. FCCPC consumer protection. NDPR for Nigerian users. ICO registration and UK GDPR for UK users. GDPR for German users - with German-specific enforcement context because German DPA is one of the most active in the EU.
Commercial: Nigerian contract law governing MSA. UK law considerations for UK enterprise clients. Cross-border DPAs for data flowing between Nigeria, Kenya and the EU.
Fundraising: SAFE mechanics reviewed against Nigerian company law for Nigerian investor rights and US securities regulation for US investor compliance. UK investor rights agreement requirements.
AI and Data: If their product uses AI - Nigerian NITDA guidance on AI. UK ICO automated decision-making guidance. German AI enforcement context.
Every gap flagged in the report is tagged to the specific country and specific legal instrument. Not "GDPR compliance issue" - but "German users: no German-language privacy notice - required under §13 TMG and enforced by Bayerisches Landesamt für Datenschutzaufsicht."
That country-level specificity is what no other platform delivers. Especially not for African countries.

Arranging The Architecture 
For your developer, here is the structure to build toward:
Phase 1 - Core countries, questionnaire-based: Nigeria, UK, USA (Delaware focus), Germany, France, Kenya, Ghana, South Africa. These are your highest-volume markets. The scan is questionnaire-based - no registry API integration yet. The scoring logic knows the legal obligations for each country and flags gaps based on the questionnaire answers.
Phase 2 - Registry API integration for accessible registries: CAC Nigeria, Companies House UK, Delaware Secretary of State, OpenCorporates for broader coverage etc. The scan pulls public data automatically before the questionnaire even starts. Company name input triggers automatic verification of existence, filing status and director records.
Phase 3 - Expanded country coverage: Add countries based on demand from pilot feedback. Each new country is a module - its own legal obligation map, its own registry reference, its own compliance framework. New countries can be added without rebuilding the platform.
Phase 4 - Investor-facing scan: Company name input. Select countries of operation. Platform runs the public data scan automatically. Investor/company/user gets a preliminary report within minutes. Full verified report with Code & Clause lawyer review within 48 hours.
